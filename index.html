    How it works

    Why hum

    Your paranasal sinuses produce nitric oxide. Humming makes the air in your nose oscillate, which swaps air between the sinuses and the nasal cavity far faster than quiet breathing. In ten healthy volunteers, nasal nitric oxide rose about 15-fold during humming compared with a quiet exhale (Weitzberg & Lundberg, 2002).

    How to hum

    • Close your lips and hum through your nose, steady and fairly strong.
    • Hold the phone 15–30 cm from your face. The iPhone microphone is on the bottom edge.
    • Tap “Hear 130 Hz” to get the pitch in your ear, then match it. No sound on iPhone? Turn off silent mode.
    • Breathe in through your nose between hums.

    How the app scores

    The app reads the fundamental pitch of your hum about 60 times a second using the YIN method. Closeness is 100 on the target and falls to 50 at 50 cents away (±3.8 Hz). A hum counts as successful when you hold it for at least a second and its average closeness is 60 or more. “On target” means within 15 cents (±1.1 Hz).

    On iPhone

    In Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, to open the tuner like an app. To stop Safari asking for the microphone every visit, tap the aA button, then Website Settings, then set Microphone to Allow.

    Audio is analyzed on your device and is never recorded or uploaded. The app measures pitch only; it can't measure nitric oxide.
